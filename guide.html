Creative EQ · User Guide

Every control,
explained.

Parameptrix is a 16-band parametric EQ with a set of filter shapes most EQs don't offer, a multiband saturator, a bit crusher, and a mode where you draw the curve and the plugin fits bands to it. This guide walks the whole plugin, in the order the signal meets it. Jump straight to Creative mode or the filter types if that's what brought you here.

Signal chain

The order things happen in.

Which is what decides how they interact.

input → [saturation, if Pre EQ] → 16 EQ bands → [saturation, if Post EQ] → bit depth → output gain → out

The analyzer's PRE tap and the meter's IN reading both come from the very start of that chain; the POST tap and the OUT reading both come from the very end, after everything.

The window

What's where.

Header

The mode tabs (Standard / Creative), the A/B slots and the preset menu. The icon at the far right opens UI size and theme.

Curve display

The frequency response from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, ±24 dB, with a numbered handle for each active band drawn over the live spectrum analyzer.

Detail strip

The selected band's FREQ, GAIN, Q and TYPE as editable fields, plus any extra control that band's type happens to have.

Band table

All sixteen slots, 1–8 on the left and 9–16 on the right.

Character panel

GLOBAL VARIANTS, SATURATION and CURVE, with the output gain and meter down the right-hand edge.

Bands

Sixteen slots.

Four are on when you insert the plugin; the rest are off and waiting. At least one band must stay active — the last one can't be switched off.

Switching on or off

Click the numbered badge in the table. A band that's off shows OFF where its gain would be.

Selecting

Click a row in the table, or a handle on the curve.

Editing on the curve

Drag a handle to set frequency and gain together. Hold Alt or Cmd while dragging to change that band's Q instead.

Ranges 20 Hz – 20 kHz

Gain ±24 dB, Q from 0.1 to 18 — 0.707 is the neutral default.

Contextual controls Three types carry one extra control each, shown in the detail strip only when that band is selected and set to that type — and each is per-band, so two Comb bands can run opposite polarities at once. Comb → POLARITY (positive or negative) · Asym → ASYM SIDE (low or high) · Formant → the vowel, chosen in the Type dropdown.

Filter types

Thirteen shapes.

Gain is greyed out on the types that don't use it, rather than doing nothing silently.
TypeGainWhat it does
PeakyesThe standard bell.
L-Shelf / H-ShelfyesLow and high shelves.
HP / LPnoHigh-pass and low-pass, 4th order. Their steepness comes from the global HIGH-PASS / LOW-PASS setting.
NotchnoA narrow removal at the centre frequency.
BPnoBand-pass — keeps a window, discards the rest.
APnoAll-pass. Flat response, phase only. Useful into a parallel path, or for smearing transients.
CombyesA feedback comb — a stack of evenly spaced teeth built from the frequency you set. Gain is how deep the teeth cut. Its polarity decides where they land: Positive puts them on the harmonics of that frequency, for a pitched, tube-like ring; Negative puts them between the harmonics, for a hollow, closed-pipe sound.
Formant · Ah Eh Ee Oh OoyesThree resonances tuned to the ratios of a sung vowel, anchored at the band's frequency. Pick the vowel in the Type dropdown — it's part of the type, not a separate control.
ResoyesA resonant peak with a sharper character than a bell. Gain sets the height of the resonance.
AsymyesA bell whose two sides differ in steepness. Its Low/High control picks which side steepens.
TiltyesRocks the whole spectrum around the band's frequency, which acts as the pivot. Positive gain lifts everything above it and drops everything below by the same amount; negative gain does the reverse.

Global variants

Settings that change a whole class of band.

HIGH-PASS / LOW-PASS — the alignment every HP and LP band uses. The two are independent, so a Sharp high-pass can run under a Gentle low-pass.

NeutralButterworth. Maximally flat, no resonance at the corner.
SharpChebyshev. Reaches its slope faster, with a small ripple and a peak just before the corner.
GentleBessel. The softest knee and the best transient behaviour; the slope arrives more gradually.

Q BEHAVIOR — how a Peak band's Q knob is read. Ignored by every other type.

Const-QThe bandwidth you set is the bandwidth you get, at any gain.
Prop.Proportional. Small boosts and cuts come out wide and gentle; large ones narrow as they grow — how most analogue EQs behave, and the more forgiving of the two when you're reaching for a big move.

Curve macros

Four knobs over every band at once.

Nothing here is destructive: put the knobs back and you have exactly the curve you built. RESET returns all four to neutral.

TUNE ±2 oct

Shifts the whole curve in frequency.

DEPTH −100 – 200 %

Scales every gain-using band's gain. 100 % is unchanged, 200 % is a caricature of the same shape, and negative values mirror the curve — every boost becomes a cut of the same size, which is a usable setting rather than a broken one.

FOCUS ±2 oct of Q

Scales every band's Q. Left is broader, right is more resonant.

SPAN ±1

Stretches or compresses the curve about a 632 Hz pivot, so the shape spreads out or gathers in around the middle.

Saturation

Four voicings, per zone or across the lot.

WarmSymmetric soft saturation, odd harmonics only.
TubeAsymmetric, adding a second harmonic under the third for a rounder result.
HardThe brightest and most aggressive of the four.
BiteClipping rather than saturation, with a mid-forward voicing of its own.

Zones LOW · MID · HIGH

Three crossover bands driven independently, or GLOBAL to drive the whole signal as one. The two are mutually exclusive — you either split the signal into zones or glue it as a whole. Press the pill under either side to switch to it.

Drive 0 – 24 dB

Input into a fixed curve. 0 dB is very nearly a wire.

MIX

The section's wet/dry, so you can blend saturation under the clean signal.

Post EQ / Pre EQ

Where the stage sits relative to the bands. Post EQ is the default and the more useful: boost a band and it drives the saturator harder, which is how a console EQ behaves.

The crossover frequencies default to 250 Hz and 2.5 kHz. They're automatable from the host's parameter list but aren't in the editor.

Bit depth

Off, 12, 8 or 4 bits.

This quantises the signal to a ladder of levels — the counterpart to saturation, where saturation bends the waveform and this one snaps it to a grid. The aliasing is deliberately not filtered out; that grit is the effect. Switching depth crossfades rather than jumping, so you can change it under a sustained note without a click.

Why there's no 16 16 bits is CD quality — it would be a setting that looks like it does something while doing nothing.

Creative mode

Draw the response, get the bands.

Switch to Creative and drag anywhere on the curve display to draw. There's no modifier and no tool to pick — the band handles are hidden in this mode, so the whole plot is your canvas.

The stroke appears as you draw it, and the plugin solves the sixteen bands when you let go, not while you're drawing. That's deliberate: re-fitting against a half-drawn curve makes the response thrash under the pointer and the sound lurch with it. The stroke is smoothed slightly before fitting, the same way a vector tool tidies a path on release. The strip under the plot reports how many bands the fit used and how far it missed your drawing by, in dB.

UNDO / REDO

Step back and forward through strokes.

RESET

Clears the drawing back to flat. This is not the CURVE macros' RESET in the character panel; it's separated from undo on purpose, since it's the more destructive of the two.

EDIT

Keep the fitted curve and go back to Standard to edit it as ordinary bands. If that wasn't what you wanted, UNDO HAND-OFF appears in Standard to take it back.

Nothing changes until you draw Entering Creative seeds the drawing from the curve that's already playing, so switching modes changes nothing you can hear.

Analyzer & meter

Watching the signal.

PRE / POST

Whether the spectrum shown is the signal arriving or the signal leaving.

FREEZE

Holds the displayed spectrum. Audio keeps flowing; only the picture stops.

Meter IN · OUT

Both readings side by side down the right-hand edge, with a hold marker at the recent peak and a numeric readout underneath. If the output clips, a latch lights across the top of the scale and stays lit until you click the meter to acknowledge it.

GAIN ±12 dB

The output gain, applied last in the chain and smoothed so you can ride it while watching the meter.

Presets & A/B

Keeping what you built.

Presets live in ~/Documents/Parameptrix/Presets as plain JSON. The dropdown in the header names the current one; its menu is where you save, and where Reveal opens the folder in Finder.

A/B — two slots holding the entire sound: every parameter, the mode you were in, and the drawn curve if there is one. Click A or B to switch between them, and A→B to copy whichever is showing over the other one — the usual way to start a variation from where you already are.

Theme & UI size

Making it fit your screen.

The icon at the far right of the header. UI size steps through 75, 100, 125, 150 and 200 %; the theme is Dark or Light. Both are per-user settings that stick across sessions rather than travelling with a preset.

Not faults

Two things that look wrong and aren't.

Gotcha Post EQ and Pre EQ sound identical until the EQ is doing something. With a flat curve the two positions are mathematically the same signal — measured bit-identical to −227 dB at any drive. The switch only bites once bands have real gain in them. Try a 12 dB boost with 12 dB of drive and the difference is obvious.
Gotcha At 4 bits the loudest peaks sit slightly lower. The quantiser's ladder stops one rung short of full scale, which makes the loudest samples up to 1.16 dB quieter than they'd otherwise be. That's deliberate: without it, a 4-bit signal pins the clip indicator permanently and the meter stops telling you anything.

Starting points

Five to try.

Vocal presence with weight

Peak at 3 kHz, +3 dB, Q around 1. Q BEHAVIOR on Prop. so it widens as you back off. Saturation on Tube, GLOBAL at 6 dB, MIX around 40 %.

Lo-fi drum bus

Bit depth 12, saturation Warm on the LOW zone at 8 dB, low-pass at 8 kHz set to Gentle.

Telephone / radio

HP at 400 Hz and LP at 3 kHz, both Sharp, then Bite on GLOBAL at 10 dB.

Vowel resonance

A Formant · Ee band around 500 Hz with +8 dB, and sweep its frequency. Then turn FOCUS up to make it ring.

Two versions of one curve

Build the EQ you want, press A→B, switch to B and pull DEPTH to −100 %. B is now the exact complement of A — the pair you want for two sides of an arrangement.
